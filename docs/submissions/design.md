# Design Event documents (Design Briefing, Design Spec Sheet, Vehicle Drawings) - S.4.2 to S.4.5, with S.4.6 to S.4.9 event-day evidence

Generated by `acceptance/submissions/make_reports.py` from the saved model. Do not edit; change the model and regenerate. The model is a candidate architecture: a link shows where a value should come from, not that the submission is complete or compliant.

The Design Event (150 of the 325 static points, S.1:p003, S.4.10.1) needs three documents uploaded before the event: a Design Briefing (PDF, on the official event template), a Design Spec Sheet (XLSX, on the provided format) and three-view Vehicle Drawings (PDF, no template; layout set by S.4.4.1). See S.4.2.1, S.4.3, S.4.4, S.4.5 and Table PS-1 rows p011-p013. The Design Judges review them before the event and use them to sort teams into design groups (S.4.2.2). Judges may also give part of the Design score to the documents themselves (S.4.9.2). Each document is uploaded as a single file into the team's own account on the Event Website by the due date posted there; the briefing and spec sheet must use the official templates with their format left unchanged (PS.1.1.1, PS.2.1, PS.2.2.1-PS.2.2.4). A file in the wrong form or format counts as Not Submitted (PS.1.2.5). A late upload costs 20 points for each day late in the 'Other' penalty group. A document still missing after the cancellation date means removal from the Design event with zero points (S.4.2.3, PS.3.2.1, PS.3.2.2, Table PS-2:p004, S.4.10.3). At the event the judges score from these documents, a conversation with team members and their own look over the finished car (S.4.9.1). The car must arrive fully built, and its tractive battery pack must already have passed inspection (S.4.6.2, IN.5.3). Rulebook S.4.3.2 hands the briefing's detailed content and per-topic slide limits to the template. Public FSAE Michigan guidance from fsaeonline.com describes the spec sheet as a two-page subsystem specification spreadsheet. It describes the briefing as a technical design-review document that judges scan across subsystems. Neither official template is reproduced here, so the briefing sections and spec-sheet fields below are derived from the rulebook's numeric limits and that public guidance. Confirm them against the 2027 templates on the Event Website. Model state: the S.4, PS.2.2, IN.5.3 and Table PS-1 design rows are in the model, but nothing in S.4 is traced to a design element yet. The model holds no numeric property values.

How to read the tables:

- **Model**: `present` means the candidate model already holds the content (an architecture element, state logic or a diagram) with nothing open; it does not mean the submitted value is final. `partial` means a model element or obligation stands for the item but holds no team value yet; items that only restate a rule value are `partial` too. `absent` means the model has nothing for the item, or cannot hold it (photos, raw test data, receipts, records).
- **Source**: where the value comes from: `model` (this Eclipse Capella® model), `cad` (CAD and drawings), `analysis` (calculations), `test` (physical tests), `supplier` (datasheets and purchase records), `admin` (team administration).
- **Status**: the team's progress. Conditional rows (aluminum, composite, monocoque, custom IA, digital APPS/BSE) count until you mark them `not_applicable` through the status CSV, once DEC-CHASSIS or the IA route is chosen ([how to update status](../../acceptance/submissions/README.md#updating-status)).
- **Blocking**: ⛔ marks a decision whose constraint name still ends in UNRESOLVED. [Open design decisions](../MODEL-GUIDE.md#open-design-decisions) lists them, and step 2 of [Using the model as a development platform](../MODEL-GUIDE.md#using-the-model-as-a-development-platform) says how to record and close one.
- **Note** says why an item is partial or absent, which rows are conditional and what is still missing. **Evidence** is the drawing, file, photo or test id the team records.
